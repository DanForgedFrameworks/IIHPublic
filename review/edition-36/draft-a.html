Welcome back

We're back, and it looks different

Heads Up! has not been through your door since November, and we would rather say why than pretend otherwise: we have had no newsletter volunteer since the end of last year.

We have used the gap to rebuild it for how most of you actually read — on a phone, in short bursts, often on a day when your head is not playing fair. Nothing on this page moves unless you ask it to, and you can change the background at the top if white is too bright.

Four new tools, not finished yet

We have rebuilt four of our most-used resources so they work on any device, read aloud properly with a screen reader, and keep working once you have opened them: What is IIH?, Family, friends and carers, the Resource Hub, and the Consensus Guidelines tool for your clinical team.

They are not finished. Before we call them done we want to hear from the people who will actually use them. About five minutes, and there are no wrong answers.

Research

The research that reaches space

Astronauts who spend months on the International Space Station can develop changes to their eyes, and to the pressure around the brain, that look remarkably like IIH.

Because of that, the team at the University of Birmingham led by Professor Alexandra Sinclair now works alongside NASA. Astronauts up there have the same kind of eye scan — an OCT — that many of you have in clinic, and what is learned in orbit feeds back into IIH care down here.

We will report properly on where it has got to in the next edition.
